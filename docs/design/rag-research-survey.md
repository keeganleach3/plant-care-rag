# RAG over-build: sourced evidence survey

Researched 2026-09-30 for the plant-care RAG planning session. Read-only; repo not inspected (project numbers below come from the task brief).

**Project (from brief):** 96 sections / 22 notes, one grower, tissue-culture aroid care. bge-base-en-v1.5, Postgres + pgvector, Claude structured output (answer + cited section ids, or fixed refusal). Eval: 38 customer-voice questions (23 dev, 15 held out); recall@k, hallucination on must-refuse, false refusals, blind human grading. Own findings: rule-based split + RRF multi-hop recall@5 0.79 to 0.88; local cross-encoder reranker recall@5 0.95 to 0.71-0.82; OR'd Postgres FTS leg (ts_rank_cd) 0.78-0.82 vs 0.95 vector alone.

## 1. Summary

Reported gains for retrieval techniques come from web-scale or long-document corpora, often against weaker baselines than bge-base: Anthropic's contextual retrieval cut top-20 retrieval failures 35% / 49% / 67% (embeddings / plus BM25 / plus reranker), LLM question decomposition added +2.9 Hits@10 points on MultiHop-RAG alone and +9.1 together with a reranker, and proposition indexing added +9 to +12 Recall@5 for unsupervised retrievers but only +2.6 to +2.8 for supervised ones (computed from the paper's table). The same literature documents the failure modes this project already hit: rerankers scored below no-reranking in 44-53% of dataset/model settings once reranking depth grew and need a domain-aligned model; semantic chunking does not reliably beat fixed-size; on fact lookup the best graph method (HippoRAG2) lands within 1.6 points of vanilla RAG with reranking while MS-GraphRAG and RAPTOR score 11-26 points below vanilla (computed); decomposition is counterproductive on already-specific queries. With vector recall@5 at 0.946 over the 14 answerable dev questions (SD 0.145, bootstrap 95% CI 0.857-1.000 from 100,000 resamples; computed by the architect from the v3 report) there is no headroom left to show more retrieval tricks, so the evidence favours over-building the test bed and the safeguards before the techniques. In order: (1) **Track C as a measurement study** (hard-distractor haystack, ANN recall vs exact, quantization retention, reranker crossover depth), because distractor type and depth flip outcomes (one hard distractor cut accuracy up to 25%; random filler raised it up to 35%) and no independent pgvector scale benchmark was opened, so the experiment itself would be the evidence; (2) **Track F**: sufficiency-gated answering (models abstain far less once context is added; gating gave +2-10% selective accuracy), judge-vs-grower agreement (LLM judges matched domain experts 60-68% vs 72-75% expert-to-expert), and paired/bootstrap reporting; (3) **Track E** as design containment plus adaptive red-teaming (PoisonedRAG 90-99% attack success from 5 injected texts; all 12 defences in the adaptive-attack study (prompting, training-based, detector and secret-knowledge types) were bypassed, most above 90%); (4) **Track A** via LLM decomposition plus a coverage/sufficiency check, consistent in size with the project's own +0.09; (5) **Track B** only as a parser bake-off on real PDFs, because no opened benchmark compares Docling / Unstructured / PyMuPDF / LlamaParse and Docling runs 0.6-1.3 pages/s on CPU with OCR over 30 s/page; (6) **Track D** last: cheap, with a plausible documented cause for the weak lexical leg (the Postgres docs say the ranking functions use no global information; IDF is not named on the page, so its absence is inference; untested here) but little headroom. Anthropic notes that a knowledge base under about 200k tokens can go whole into the prompt instead of through retrieval; this corpus is almost certainly below that (inference: it would need over 2,000 tokens per section to exceed it), so any "scale" claim needs a whole-corpus-in-prompt control arm and a synthetic growth corpus.

### Conflicts between sources

- **Reranking:** Cohere reranker took Anthropic's top-20 failure from 2.9% to 1.9% and BEIR ranks rerankers best zero-shot on average, but Drowning in Documents finds degradation with depth and the project's own reranker lowered recall. Settings differ (commercial reranker and large corpora vs a local cross-encoder on 96 sections; candidate counts were not captured for Anthropic), so both can hold; the crossover is unmeasured.
- **Chunking:** Qu et al. and Chroma find no reliable semantic-chunking gain; HiChunk reports +6.97 Evidence Recall on its own HiCBench and +1.03 on Qasper with a fine-tuned LLM chunker plus Auto-Merge. Different method, authors' own benchmark on one of the two sets.
- **Late chunking vs contextual retrieval:** Jina's paper reports nDCG gains of 1.5-1.9 points; the independent study (abstract only, no numbers) says contextual retrieval keeps coherence better at higher compute. A trade-off, not a contradiction.
- **Noise:** Power of Noise finds random filler helps (up to +35%) while hard distractors hurt (up to -25% from one document); Drowning in Documents finds deeper reranking hurts. Generator-side vs reranker-side effects; the haystack should contain both kinds.
- **LLM-judge validity:** RAGAS reports 0.95 faithfulness agreement on 50 general Wikipedia questions; Szymanski et al. find 60-68% with domain experts.
- **GraphRAG:** Microsoft reports 72-83% comprehensiveness win rates vs vector RAG on global questions; GraphRAG-Bench finds vanilla ties on fact retrieval. Different question types; reconcilable.
- **Agents:** +90.2% on an internal research eval comes with about 15x tokens; the gain is unverified outside Anthropic's eval.
