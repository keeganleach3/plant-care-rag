## First substrate mix by genus

The first potting mix for tissue-culture Monstera and Anthurium is tree fern fiber and perlite at about 45 percent each, plus about 10 percent coco coir. For Alocasia and Philodendron it is the same mix with about 20 percent coco coir, with the tree fern and perlite each dropping slightly to make room. The only goal of the change is a bit more dense material for fine roots to pull water from. Any plant with very fine, thin roots gets the denser mix, because fine roots need less airflow and need to be able to grab water from the substrate. A mix that is too dense is one of the most common beginner mistakes.

## Water pH for the first watering

Water is balanced to a pH of about 7, or the high sixes. Plain tap water from the sink is fine; distilled water is not needed and did not improve success rates.

## What goes in the first watering

For the first watering, per half liter of water: one dropper of water-soluble IBA rooting hormone, a small amount of a high-phosphorus fertilizer, and Trichoderma harzianum T-22. Water the freshly potted plantlets with this once. Under a 100 percent humidity dome it is usually at least ten days before they need water again.

## Second-stage mix after acclimation: chunky aroid soil

Past the first mix, plants move to a chunky aroid soil: coco coir, perlite, charcoal, coconut husk chips, and a bit of orchid bark. Monstera and Anthurium get the chunkiest version because they love airflow around the roots. Alocasia and Philodendron get the same ingredients with slightly more fine material and coco coir to hold water, because their finer roots struggle in a mix that is too chunky. A very large Alocasia with thick roots can eventually move to a chunkier mix, but that is far in the future.

## Top watering after the dome: water until it drains

Once a plant is past the dome and not on a wick, it is top watered. Water until a little runs out of the bottom of the pot every time, so the whole pot is evenly saturated, then let it drain fully. Do not leave it soaked; the goal is a complete, even watering, not a heavy one.

## Light fertilizing with every watering

Every watering carries a light dose of fertilizer, for all plants, and this produced a large increase in growth. The other levers for a plant that has stalled are more light and a consistent routine.
