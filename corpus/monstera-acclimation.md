## Which Monstera this covers

Tissue-culture Monstera here are mostly Monstera deliciosa and Monstera borsigiana, and almost all variegated: Thai Constellation, albo, white monster, bulbosa, devil monster, green on green, aurea, and many more. All of them are handled the same way. Monstera adansonii has not been grown from tissue culture here, so nothing in this corpus covers it.

## Monstera tolerate a faster humidity step-down

Monstera are faster growers and more tolerant of problems than Alocasia or Anthurium. Once a Monstera is acclimated to 75 to 80 percent humidity and is more than a few inches tall, roughly in a three-inch pot, it can go straight into 50 percent humidity without issues. Alocasia need a much slower step-down and should not be moved like this.

## Monstera root faster and need less water

Monstera usually root faster than other genera, so they need to size up sooner. They are also more resilient to drought, so they are watered less often than Alocasia or Anthurium.

## What a TC Monstera should look like at week one

At week one the plantlet should look about the same as when it was potted, just a little perkier. A plant that flopped because it grew crooked in the bag is fine as long as it feels firm in the pot. A plant that moves around a lot when touched needs checking.

## What a TC Monstera should look like at week three

By week three there should be multiple visible roots in a clear pot, or at least one piece of new growth, often a couple. In a one-and-a-half-inch starter pot this is usually up-pot time; in a larger pot it will not be yet.
