## Domes and trays used here

Several kinds of humidity domes are in use: AC Infinity domes with grow lights built into the lid, and domes from Greenhouse Megastore over 72-cell trays. Any of them work; the dome is what lets the tray hold 100 percent humidity for the first week.

## Cabinets instead of humidifiers

Plants past the dome live in retrofitted IKEA glass cabinets (Fabrikör and Milsbo) and a Barrina LP900 grow cabinet. No humidifier is used anywhere: the plants and the enclosed cabinet hold the humidity on their own at the 65 to 80 percent the grow space runs at. Cabinets are the recommendation for anyone wanting to raise humidity, and they pair naturally with LED light bars mounted inside.

## Airflow inside a cabinet

Small fans sit in the corners of each cabinet to get a circular air movement going. Strength is not the point; the "leaves barely move" test from the airflow notes still applies.

## Hygrometers

Any cheap hygrometer from Amazon is fine for reading cabinet or tent humidity. Nothing special is needed.

## Advice for a dry home with no cabinet

For a customer whose house sits around 40 percent humidity: group plants together so they raise the humidity around each other, consider a cabinet with light bars for both humidity and light, and when a new plant first arrives, cover it in a clear plastic bag and slowly expose it to more air over time so it adjusts to the room's ambient humidity instead of being shocked by it.

## Condensation under domes is not a problem

Condensation and standing water under a dome are not something to worry about when planting tissue-culture plantlets. It goes away on its own as the dome is opened further during the humidity step-down.
