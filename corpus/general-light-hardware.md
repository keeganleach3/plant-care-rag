## Full-spectrum white LED bars are the recommendation

Every fixture used here is an LED bar. Bars are the most useful form and the easiest to place around a shelf or tent; large full panels are overkill for these builds and do not penetrate as far down. One hanging bulb is used only because it was convenient. The light must be full spectrum; that is the thing to check when a customer asks which grow light to buy. Color does not matter in practice: white works fine, and while pink or red-blue lights might show a statistical difference if you dug deep, a balanced white light is the choice here.

## The Barrina T5 is weak: the numbers

The Barrina T5 is the most common cheap grow light customers own. It is full spectrum, but a single bar puts out very little usable light and it falls off fast with distance. Per its product chart, measured at the center of the bar, one bar delivers about 91 PPFD at 8 inches, 59.3 at 12 inches, and 31.2 at 20 inches. At 8 inches, which is quite close, for 12 hours a day, that is a DLI of 3.93, below even the low-light band. To make a T5 setup work, stack several bars side by side or buy a stronger fixture.

## The stronger fixtures used here

The grow tents run either AC Infinity S16 light bars, which use Samsung LED diodes and carry light much further down so it dissipates more slowly, or the thicker Barrina TXS45A bars. AC Infinity does not publish PPFD figures, and the S16 can be set to multiple levels, so its output is hard to judge. Its bars use Samsung LM301H EVO LEDs, rated at 3.14 µmol/J PPE, and a set of four IONBEAM S16 bars is listed at 42 watts. The Barrina TXS45A listing (135 W, three 45 W bars, CRI 98) shows, per light and measured at the center of the bar, 429.7 PPFD at 3.94 inches, 216.9 at 7.87 inches, and 137 at 11.81 inches, which is about 2.4 times a Barrina T5 at the same 8-inch distance. A few inches from the plants these fixtures deliver roughly 350 PPFD, which over 12 hours is a DLI of about 15. That is too much for a very small plant, but small plants are easy to set further from the fixture, and as they grow they move closer and their PPFD rises on its own.

## Estimating AC Infinity S16 light from its watts

AC Infinity publishes no PPFD chart for the S16, so its light is estimated from power. A set of four IONBEAM S16 bars draws 42 watts at level 10, and power scales with the level: about 29.4 watts at level 7 and 21 watts at level 5. Multiplying the watts by the LM301H EVO rating of 3.14 µmol/J gives the light the bars put out: about 132 µmol/s at level 10, 92 at level 7, and 66 at level 5. Dividing that by the lit area in square meters gives the average PPFD across the area: over a 2 × 2 ft space (0.37 m²) about 355 at level 10, 248 at level 7, and 177 at level 5; over a 2 × 4 ft space (0.74 m²) about 177, 124, and 89. These are estimates from the LED rating, not measurements, and they are averages across the whole area, while the Barrina charts measure the center of the bar, where light is strongest.

## Where to put each plant under a bar

A light bar is strongest directly under its center and weaker toward its ends, so PPFD drops as you move a plant toward the sides. The most light-hungry plants go in the center: variegated Monstera and Alocasia get the most light here. Anything that wants less light, or is getting too much under a strong fixture, goes toward the side or further away.

## Photoperiod used here

All established plants get at least 12 hours of light a day. Acclimating plantlets get 10 to 12, sometimes 12. The schedule is never shortened.
