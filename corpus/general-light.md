## Established plants: at least 250 PPFD for 12 to 14 hours

Once past acclimation, all of these aroids, and variegated forms especially, want a lot of light. Grow lights here sit very close to the plants and run twelve to fourteen hours a day, with the plants getting at least 250 PPFD and ideally 300 or more. Acclimating plantlets use a lower target of around 150 PPFD; see the acclimation protocol.

## Light intensity drops off fast with distance

Usable light drops off with distance from the fixture far faster than people expect. On a single Barrina T5, the product chart shows 91 PPFD at 8 inches, 59.3 at 12 inches, and 31.2 at 20 inches. It also drops toward the ends of a bar compared with directly under its center. Cheaper fixtures and cheaper LED nodes make both effects worse.

## Cheap grow lights work only up close

Cheap grow lights are not inherently wrong. Barrina T5 fixtures are very popular with customers but very weak, and off-brand lights give roughly what you pay for. Plants under them need to be kept very close to the fixture, or several bars stacked together.

## Signs of too little light

Slow growth, leggy growth, and leaves that stay small instead of sizing up. A plant that is growing slowly, stretching, and not producing big full leaves is almost always short on light.

## Signs of too much light

A plant that seems to be leaning away from the light, or develops a yellowish burned area on the leaf, is getting light burn, the indoor version of sunburn. Fixtures can also run hot, and a leaf touching a hot fixture will burn; it has not happened here but it can.

## The most common lighting mistake

Without doubt: not giving plants enough light. Blast them with light. They appreciate it and grow faster for it.
