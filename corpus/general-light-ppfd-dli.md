## What PPFD means

PPFD stands for photosynthetic photon flux density. In the simplest terms it is the light a plant can see: the amount of usable light that actually lands on the surface of the leaf, which is different from the brightness our eyes see. It is measured in micromoles per square meter per second (µmol/m²/s). Grow-light listings also quote lumens, lux, or just "strong," and those numbers mean different things; online calculators convert between them, but PPFD is the one to compare.

## What DLI means and the three light bands

DLI (daily light integral) is PPFD multiplied by the hours the light is on, so it captures how much light a plant gets over a whole day rather than at one instant. Addition (standard formula, matches the Waveform Lighting DLI calculator at waveformlighting.com/horticulture/daily-light-integral-dli-calculator): DLI = PPFD × hours × 3600 ÷ 1,000,000. By that calculator's bands, low-light plants need a DLI of 5 to 10, medium-light plants 10 to 15, and high-light plants more than 15.

## Aroids are high-light plants, whatever the store label says

Big-box labels call tropical aroids medium- or low-light plants. Indoors, they almost never are. These are not outdoor plants unless they are slowly acclimated to sun, but as indoor plants they need more light than people think. Every aroid grown here is treated as a high-light plant with a target DLI of 15 or more, and established plants are given at least 250 PPFD [CONFIRM: session 2 said "at least 300 PPFD"; which figure stands?]. Tissue-culture plantlets start lower, around 100 to 150 PPFD [CONFIRM: session 1 said 150 to 200], and pick up more light as they grow toward the fixture.

## How to measure light at the plant

Most fixtures publish PPFD at several distances in their listing images, and that chart is the first place to look. To measure directly, a cheap lux meter from Amazon plus an online lux-to-PPFD calculator gives a workable PPFD estimate at the leaf.
