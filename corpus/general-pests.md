## Root rot is the biggest risk for small plants

Root rot is the main problem across all genera, especially while plants are small and held in high humidity. The cause is almost always too little airflow under the dome or lid. With Alocasia, watch for fungus as well, for the same reason: give them a good amount of airflow. On an established plant, rot looks the same as overwatering: yellowing, wilting, droopiness, browning edges, and mushy sections. Directly after planting from tissue culture the only early sign is usually a loss of firmness, and by the time leaves die it is too late.

## Thrips: rotate sprays every four days, or use systemic granules

Thrips adapt to pesticides quickly and lay eggs in the soil, so treatment must be consistent and rotated between different pesticides. When thrips are found, spray every four days, mostly with a spinosad-based pesticide. Where it is legal, systemic granules (imidacloprid or a similar active ingredient) watered into the soil protect the plant for a month or more; the plant takes the chemical up and pests that bite it die. Do not use systemic granules if spider mites are present: they have been found to speed up spider-mite reproduction rather than kill them, so an infestation can spread faster.

## Spider mites: sprays plus wiping

Spider mites are an easier kill than thrips. Neem oil, spinosad, or most other pesticide sprays work, combined with manually wiping the leaves. Wipe both sides of each leaf.

## Mealybugs: oil sprays and alcohol swabs

Oil-based sprays, homemade or store-bought, work on mealybugs. Removing them by hand with a rubbing-alcohol swab is very effective and easy.

## Fungus gnats: treat premixed soil the day you use it

Any premixed potting soil or similar product should be treated for fungus gnats immediately on first use, before an infestation starts. The method: soak Mosquito Bits (the brand) in the water you are about to water with for a few hours, then water with it. This kills the larvae in the soil far more efficiently than the usual published methods like spraying oil on the surface or covering the top of the soil.

## Bugs under the dome: leave springtails, treat pests gently at night

Bugs are not common while plantlets are still under the dome, and what to do depends on the bug. Most likely it is springtails or soil mites; if so, leave them, because they are beneficial. A harmful pest such as thrips or spider mites must be treated, along with all the plants around it, but plantlets are extremely fragile at that stage, which puts you in a difficult position. A plantlet cannot be sprayed and then put under grow lights: it will most likely burn and die. Spray gently at night so the leaves can dry off.

## Pesticide and fungicide products: follow the bottle

Pesticides and fungicides here are bought premixed, or diluted per the instructions on each bottle. Products come from several brands on Amazon and every one dilutes differently, so there is no single dilution rule; read the label of the product in hand.

## Beneficial insects are not used here

Beneficial insects have not been used here. Growers who tried them found they sounded better in theory than they worked in practice: they take too long, they maintain a pest population instead of stopping the spread, and you cannot spray pesticides while they are present without killing them. Pesticides kill the pests and they stay gone.
